Robotic Ball Interception and Targeting

ME/CS/EE 133 Robotics Project

Project Overview

For this project, my team developed a robotic system that uses a Franka Panda arm to intercept a falling ball and hit it toward a randomly generated target. The robot must predict where and when the ball can be reached, move the paddle smoothly to the intercept point, and orient the paddle so that the ball leaves in the direction of the goal. Because the ball and goal positions change each round, the robot must create a new plan for every attempt.

System Design

We modeled the motion of the ball under gravity and predicted the time at which it would reach a feasible interception height. The controller then calculated the required paddle position, orientation, and velocity. A momentum-based collision model was used to relate the motion of the paddle to the desired outgoing velocity of the ball.

The Franka Panda's seven revolute joints were combined with two additional prismatic joints at the paddle, giving the system nine degrees of freedom. The additional joints expanded the usable paddle area and helped the robot reach more possible ball locations. We also modified the robot's URDF and paddle geometry so that the visual model, kinematics, and collision surface were correctly aligned.

Motion Planning and Control

  • Predicted the ball's future position and velocity using projectile motion.
  • Calculated the paddle normal and launch velocity needed to send the ball toward the goal.
  • Used iterative inverse kinematics with a weighted damped pseudoinverse to solve for the robot configuration.
  • Applied null-space projection so the secondary joint-centering objective did not interfere with the main interception task.
  • Generated smooth joint motion with cubic splines for both the strike and return trajectories.
  • Checked workspace, timing, and convergence limits before executing each plan.

My Contribution

I worked with my teammates on the system modeling, inverse-kinematics implementation, trajectory planning, collision calculations, testing, and analysis. The project brought together robot kinematics, dynamics, numerical methods, task-space control, and simulation in one complete system.

Skills: Python, robotic kinematics, inverse kinematics, Jacobians, trajectory planning, cubic splines, collision modeling, URDF editing, simulation, and task-space control
